In short

What is family enterprise planning?

Family enterprise planning is the process of deciding, documenting and rehearsing how leadership and ownership of a family-owned company pass to the next generation. It covers three handovers that are often confused: management, ownership, and the family authority behind both. A complete plan names successors, sets a timetable, protects and transfers assets, and defines how the family will resolve disagreement.

Common questions

Asked on the first call.

When should a family business start succession planning?

While the founder still has the authority to make an unpopular decision stick. Once illness, a dispute or an outside offer sets the timetable, the range of workable options has already narrowed.

How is this different from estate planning?

Estate planning decides how assets pass and what tax is due. Succession planning decides who runs and who owns the business. Families that do one without the other find the gap at the worst moment.

How long does it take?

About 196 hours of facilitated work, roughly 24.5 days, from the first family orientation to the ceremonial signing of the plan. See the full breakdown in our FAQs.
