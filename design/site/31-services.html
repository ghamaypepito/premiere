The rules a family agrees to while everyone is still reasonable, so that the one year they are not, the rules are already there.

What is a family constitution?

A family constitution is a written agreement among the members of a business-owning family that sets out how they will make decisions together. It typically covers who may work in the business and on what terms, how shares may be sold and to whom, how dividends are decided, how the board and the family council are composed, and how disputes are resolved. It is not usually a contract enforceable in court. Its authority comes from having been negotiated openly and signed by everyone it binds.

What we build

Three structures, and the habits that keep them alive.

Most families already have one of these on paper. Very few have all three meeting on a calendar.

—The family council. Where the family speaks as owners, separately from the dinner table
—The board. Real agendas, real minutes, and at least one director who is not a relative
—The family constitution. Employment, dividends, share transfers, exit and dispute resolution
—A family employment policy. The document that prevents ten future arguments
—A meeting calendar. Quarterly, in the diary, with someone accountable for chairing it

Governance is what makes a succession stick.

Professionalizing a family business without losing the family

Roles, systems and accountability that outlast the founder, installed without stripping out the thing that made the company work in the first place.

What does professionalizing a family business mean?

Professionalizing a family business means replacing decisions that depend on one person's memory and presence with systems anyone competent can run. In practice it covers defined roles and reporting lines, written policies for hiring and promotion that apply to relatives too, financial reporting the family can read, delegated authority with spending limits, and performance review for family and non-family staff on the same terms. The test is simple: if the founder were unreachable for a month, what would stop?

Where it usually starts

The founder is the bottleneck, and everybody knows it.

—Every cheque above a small amount still needs one signature
—Two relatives hold the same title and neither knows who decides
—The best non-family manager has no path above their current role
—The monthly figures arrive late, and nobody fully trusts them
—Nothing is written down, because it has never needed to be

A business that only runs on one person cannot be handed to anyone.

Preparing the next generation to lead

The successor is usually ready in ability long before they are ready in authority. Those are two different problems and only one of them is solved by time.

How do you prepare a successor in a family business?

Preparing a successor means building three things in parallel: competence, credibility and authority. Competence comes from real profit-and-loss responsibility, ideally including work outside the family firm. Credibility comes from non-family staff seeing the successor earn results that were not handed to them. Authority comes from the founder publicly and repeatedly transferring decisions, not merely titles. A successor given authority without credibility inherits a company that will not follow them.

What we run

A development plan per person, not a course.

Siblings and cousins are rarely on the same path, and pretending otherwise is how resentment starts.

—An honest assessment of readiness, given privately first
—A staged plan with real responsibility and real consequences
—Outside experience where it would help, and a route back
—Coaching for the founder on letting go, which is the harder half
—A defined role for members who will own but never manage

Prepare the successor before the handover, not during it.
