What is family business succession planning?

Family business succession planning is the process of deciding, documenting and rehearsing how leadership and ownership of a family-owned company will pass to the next generation. It covers three separate handovers that are often confused: management, ownership, and the family authority that sits behind both. A complete plan names successors, sets a timetable, values and transfers shares, and defines how the family will resolve disagreement afterwards.

Who this is for

Three situations, three different pieces of work.

The founder is ready, the family is not

A capable successor exists, but the siblings, the spouse, or the shares have never been discussed openly.

The family is ready, the founder is not

The next generation has come back, trained and waiting. The handover keeps moving one more year.

Nobody is ready and time is short

Illness, a sudden death, or a buyer at the door. The work compresses and the sequence changes.

Common questions

The questions families ask on the first call.

When should a family business start succession planning?

The useful answer is not an age, it is a condition: start while the founder still has the authority to make an unpopular decision stick. Once health, a dispute or an outside offer forces the timetable, the family is negotiating under pressure and the range of workable options has already narrowed.

What is the difference between succession planning and estate planning?

Succession planning decides who runs and who owns the business. Estate planning decides how assets pass and what tax is due. A family that does one without the other usually discovers the gap at the worst possible moment, when the shares have transferred cleanly but nobody has the authority to run what they now own.

What if the next generation does not want the business?

That is a legitimate answer and it is better heard early than discovered late. The work then shifts to separating ownership from management: professional leadership running the company, the family holding and governing it, and a clear route for anyone who wants to exit their stake without breaking the business apart.

How do you keep the process confidential inside one family?

Individual interviews stay individual. What comes back to the family is the pattern, never the transcript, and never attributed. Nothing is recorded. The firm is retained by the family as a whole rather than by the founder, so no single member can direct what the others are told.
